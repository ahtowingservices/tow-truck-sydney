What to Do When Your Car Needs Towing: A Practical Guide

A car that suddenly stops working can leave you unsure about what to do next. Whether the problem is mechanical, electrical or caused by accident damage, knowing how to handle the situation can help protect you, your passengers and your vehicle.

Not every vehicle can be safely driven to a mechanic. When continuing to drive could cause further damage or create a safety problem, arranging professional towing may be the more practical option.

Recognise When Your Car Should Be Towed

Some problems may appear minor but can make a vehicle unsafe to drive. If the engine has stopped, the vehicle is overheating, the steering or braking system is affected, or the car has suffered significant accident damage, driving it further may not be appropriate.

Towing can also be useful when a vehicle will not start, has been left with a serious mechanical fault, or needs to be transported to a workshop without being driven on the road.

Move to a Safe Position If Possible

If your vehicle breaks down while you are travelling, safety should be your first priority. If the car can be moved safely, position it away from moving traffic and switch on the hazard lights.

Avoid standing beside the vehicle in a dangerous traffic area. If you are travelling with passengers, keep everyone in a safe location while waiting for assistance whenever conditions allow.

Give the Towing Operator Accurate Information

When arranging a tow, clear information helps the operator understand what type of vehicle and equipment may be involved. Tell them your location and explain what happened to the car.

Useful information includes:

  • Vehicle make and model
  • Exact pickup location
  • Whether the vehicle starts
  • Whether the wheels can roll
  • Whether the vehicle has accident damage
  • Whether the vehicle is parked in a difficult position
  • Your preferred destination

If you are unsure about the mechanical problem, simply describe the symptoms. You do not need to diagnose the vehicle yourself.

Choose the Right Towing Equipment

Different vehicles and situations may require different towing methods. A standard car that can roll normally may be handled differently from a damaged vehicle with steering, suspension or wheel problems.

For vehicles that require careful loading, suitable towing equipment can help reduce the risk of additional damage during transportation. Providing accurate information before the tow allows the operator to prepare for the situation.

Prepare Your Vehicle Before It Is Towed

Before the vehicle is loaded, remove valuable personal belongings where possible. Check the cabin, boot and storage compartments for items that you do not want to leave inside the vehicle.

If you have the vehicle keys available, keep them ready for the towing operator. If the car has an alarm, electronic parking brake or other system that may affect loading, mention it before the vehicle is moved.

Know Where the Vehicle Is Going

Decide where you want the vehicle transported before the tow begins. Depending on the situation, this could be a mechanic, repair centre, dealership, private property or another suitable destination.

Confirm the destination address carefully so there is no confusion when the vehicle arrives. If the destination has restricted access or specific delivery instructions, provide those details in advance.

Ask About the Towing Cost

Towing prices can vary depending on factors such as distance, vehicle type, time of service, location and the type of equipment required. Asking what is included before the tow starts can help you understand the expected cost.

If additional work may be required because of accident damage, difficult access or a vehicle that cannot roll, ask about this before confirming the service.

What to Do After an Accident

After a collision, check for immediate safety concerns and follow any instructions from emergency services or other relevant authorities. Do not attempt to drive a vehicle that has suffered damage affecting its safety or roadworthiness.

When arranging recovery, explain the visible damage and whether any wheels, doors or other parts are affected. This information can help the towing operator prepare appropriately.

Why Planning Ahead Helps

Most people do not expect their vehicle to break down, but keeping basic emergency information available can make an unexpected situation easier to manage. Save important contact details on your phone and know your vehicle's make, model and registration details.

It is also useful to know the location of your nearest preferred mechanic or repair facility so you can provide a destination quickly if towing becomes necessary.

Final Thoughts

When a vehicle cannot be safely driven, arranging a suitable tow can help you move it without adding unnecessary risk or damage. The most important steps are to stay safe, provide accurate information, choose appropriate equipment and confirm the destination and expected charges.
